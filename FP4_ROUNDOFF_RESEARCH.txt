FP4 roundoff in the compact two-flavour HMC workflow
===================================================
Research demo, 32^4 on DGX Spark. Target memory budget: 128^4.
No QUDA dependency. This explores numerical methods, not validated sampling.

1. Problem and mathematical model
---------------------------------
The even-parity system is A(U)x=phi, A=M^dagger M,
M=I-kappa^2 D_eo D_oe. In exact arithmetic this is Hermitian positive definite
when M is nonsingular. Our implemented map Ahat is nonlinear: its four Dslash
stages, identity subtractions and vector updates round to FP4.

Write Q(z)=z+e(z). For a computed CG iteration,
  x_next = x + alpha*p + eta_x
  r_next = r - alpha*(A*p + epsilon_p) + eta_r.
The gap g=(phi-A*x)-r therefore evolves as
  g_next = g + alpha*epsilon_p - A*eta_x - eta_r.
A shrinking recursive residual does not establish that phi-A*x is shrinking.
Conjugacy also degrades. This is why we measure both recursive and recomputed
residuals and use an independent diagnostic operator.

The original six-real row codec stores three E2M1 bytes and one E4M3 scale:
  Q(z)=s*RN_E2M1(z/s),
  s=2^ceil(log2(max(maxabs(z)/6, 2^-9))).
The minimum positive magnitude is 2^-10 at the smallest scale. Near a value
of one, representable FP4 spacings are orders of magnitude larger than dt=1e-3.
Increasing volume does not fix deterministic rounding of a small update.

2. Proposed treatments and their implementations
------------------------------------------------
A. Hierarchical scaling / normalization
Let c approximate the inverse RMS of the RHS, using a power of two to avoid
additional rounding. Solve A*y=c*phi and decode x=y/c. Store y and all its
work vectors at the same scale; compare physical residuals to ||phi||.
Our demo stores this single global gain in Expansion.gain. The small-RHS
ablation compares gains 1 and 65536 for RHS amplitude 1e-5. This extends range;
it does not create more FP4 mantissa bits or cure operator bias by itself.
RMS normalization is preferable to unit-L2 normalization here: unit L2 makes
individual entries decay as 1/sqrt(volume), approaching the codec's scale floor.

B. Two-component FP4 representation (explicitly more than four effective bits)
  hi = Q(c*z)
  lo = Q(256*(c*z-hi))
  z_tilde = (hi+lo/256)/c.
A decoded tile is updated once and re-split, carrying small corrections in lo
without a full FP32 master array. Subject to range/no-clipping assumptions,
second quantization acts on the first rounding error, producing an O(u^2)
error instead of O(u), plus scale-floor errors. This is an error expansion,
not an implementation of ECO's optimizer and not an exact arithmetic guarantee.

The native kernel evaluates D(hi)+D(lo)/256 with two FP4 tensor-core products,
FP32 register accumulation and immediate splitting of the output into two FP4
components. Every Schur stage keeps the correction, including identity-minus-
hopping cancellation. Storage is eight bytes per six reals (~10.67 bits/real),
versus four bytes for the original FP4 codec and twelve for FP16. Thus improved
accuracy has a stated memory/computation cost; we do not call it free FP4.

C. Identity-centered gauge factorization
  U = I + B,  B_tilde=Q(128*B)/128.
The field stores B rather than U. A small off-diagonal link update no longer
shares an FP4 scale with a diagonal entry near one. Eight transport directions
cache B or B^dagger, and the native operator explicitly evaluates
  (I+B)x = x + Bx.
Simply storing centered links but repacking I+B into the old cache would lose
the improvement; our kernel and refresh path preserve the factorization.
This representation works algebraically for general U, but its fixed gain and
rounding have only been tested here on cold/near-identity fields. It does not
preserve SU(3) exactly; measured unitarity defects are reported. A moving local
reference U=U_ref exp(X), or compact Lie-algebra coordinates, is a future way to
cover general fields without relying on near-identity scales. Not implemented.

D. Momentum corrections with compact eight-coordinate blocks
Momentum has eight Lie-algebra coordinates per link. Represent each coordinate
with two FP4 components, using one scale per eight coefficients: 4+1 bytes per
component, 10 bytes/link total. This avoids the original padding to twelve
reals. Only the decoded tile has padding for interface compatibility. At 128^4
it costs 10 GiB, versus 8 GiB for the old padded single-component layout and
16 GiB for a naive padded two-component layout.

E. Recompute/restart the residual periodically
After every m iterations, recompute r=phi-Ahat(x) and restart p=r, retaining the
fixed outer iteration count. This controls recurrence drift at the cost of an
extra native application; it does not remove Ahat's bias. The ablation includes
24 iterations both with and without refresh, so the extra iteration budget is
not confused with the effect of refreshing. Default existing HMC behavior is
unchanged unless residual_refresh_interval is set.

F. Stochastic rounding as a bounded experiment
For adjacent levels a<=z<=b, choose b with probability (z-a)/(b-a), else a.
Without saturation this preserves expectation, but increases variance. Our
experiment stochastically encodes persistent solver-state updates; native
operator outputs remain deterministic. This is NOT full stochastic HMC or
NVIDIA's full training recipe. Small bias and range tests accompany the code.
It performed worse in the tested solver configuration; that is not a universal
claim about stochastic rounding.

G. Hamiltonian accumulation
Normalized energy density h=H/V is useful for interpretation, but the correct
Metropolis exponent remains -DeltaH=-V*Delta h. Dividing the acceptance exponent
by volume would change the target distribution. Neither normalization nor a
small-looking density repairs accumulated rounding bias.
The optional --scalar64 demo accumulates gauge, kinetic and fermion energy
reductions in FP64 scalars/register reductions; volume-sized arrays and local
products remain reduced precision / FP32. CG arithmetic is unchanged. This
reduces summation and final-subtraction error but cannot repair local FP32
plaquette cancellation, inaccurate solves or quantized gauge dynamics. Paired
local energy differences would further reduce cancellation; not implemented.

3. Mapping to every workflow stage
----------------------------------
Save/restore: preserve packed bits and representation/gain metadata, not decoded
masters. ResearchHMC.checkpoint/restore has a distinct research-fp4-v1 schema.
Momentum refresh: compact two-component coordinates reduce Gaussian and update
quantization; this is still a quantized noise distribution.
Noise/pseudofermion refresh: two-component vectors and Schur stages reduce input
and transform error; tests record the RHS representation error separately.
Initial/final energy solve: scaled RHS, expanded vectors/operator, optional
residual refresh; measure independent residual as well as recurrence residual.
Force solves: same treatments, with fixed budgets and explicit diagnostic cost.
Force/kicks: tilewise FP32 differentiation of the smooth surrogate; retain the
momentum correction after each kick. Expansion does not prove force consistency.
Gauge drift: centered state and centered native transport cache preserve small
updates. Record changed-link count, RMS movement and unitarity defect.
Hamiltonian evaluation: stage before/after H and components, optionally wider
scalar reductions. Report total DeltaH and density; acceptance uses total.
Accept/reject: retain standard exp(-DeltaH), restore exact encoded state on
rejection. No claim of detailed balance for this approximate dynamics.
Checkpoint: representation, dimensions, parameters and CUDA RNG persisted;
research format deliberately distinct from the original executable's format.

4. Literature grounding (primary sources, accessed 2026-10-28)
-------------------------------------------------------------
[1] NVIDIA et al., Pretraining Large Language Models with NVFP4,
    arXiv:2509.25149v2 (2026-03-04 revision; first submitted 2025).
    https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.25149v2
    Uses block scaling, rotations, stochastic rounding and selective wider
    precision. This supports exploring a recipe rather than assuming FP4 casts
    alone suffice. Its LLM results do not validate our HMC sampler.
[2] NVIDIA Transformer Engine, NVFP4 documentation (development documentation).
    https://nvidia.github.io/TransformerEngine/features/low_precision_training/nvfp4/nvfp4.html
    Describes global FP32 and local E4M3 scaling and selective stochastic
    rounding. Our six-real blocks and power-of-two scales differ from NVFP4's
    standard sixteen-value blocks. The global-gain experiment follows the same
    range-extension principle, not a drop-in Transformer Engine kernel.
[3] Nikdan, Zandieh, Alistarh, Mirrokni, ECO: Quantized Training without
    Full-Precision Master Weights, arXiv:2601.22101v1 (2026-01-29).
    https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.22101v1
    Carries quantization error through optimizer momentum instead of storing
    master weights. This motivates retained corrections. Our explicit second
    component consumes extra memory, unlike ECO's stated no-extra-memory
    optimizer transformation; its convergence proof does not apply to CG/HMC.
[4] Panferov, Schultheis, Tabesh, Alistarh, Quartet II: Accurate LLM Pre-Training
    in NVFP4 by Improved Unbiased Gradient Estimation,
    arXiv:2601.22813v2 (2026-06-01).
    https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.22813v2
    MS-EDEN combines rotations with stochastic scale correction; it illustrates
    the distinction between low MSE and unbiasedness. We did not implement
    MS-EDEN or its guarantees. Rotating our six-real color blocks would require
    transforming gauge, spin and boundary operations consistently; the exact
    identity-plus-correction split was a simpler first experiment.

5. Scope and reproducibility
----------------------------
run_fp4_roundoff.sh launches demo_fp4_roundoff.py in the pinned existing image.
Defaults: 32^4, twelve solver iterations, tile 8192. --warm generates deterministic
near-identity SU(3) links with Lie-algebra coefficient scale .05, not a thermalized
ensemble. All modes see the same raw Gaussian RHS before their chosen encoding.
Independent residual uses decoded gauge, FP32 tile arithmetic, FP16 intermediate
storage; it measures error relative to that decoded gauge, not an original
unquantized physical configuration. Tiny-lattice validation compares this oracle
against the fully differentiable FP32 network. It is not an FP64 oracle.
The early smoke.json predates the FP16 diagnostic and is not comparative evidence.

Example:
  ./run_fp4_roundoff.sh --mode center_dual --warm --iters 24 --restart-every 4 \
    --output /work/benchmark-results/fp4-roundoff/custom-solver.json
  ./run_fp4_roundoff.sh --trajectory --mode center_dual_mom --scalar64 --seed 45 \
    --output /work/benchmark-results/fp4-roundoff/custom-trajectory.json
  ./run_fp4_roundoff_tests.sh

Full trajectories use one leapfrog step, dt=.001, four iterations per solve.
Hamiltonian diagnostics add separate solves. Motion diagnostics retain a packed
before-drift copy; measured peak includes it. Trajectory timings include tracing
and first-use overhead; solver timings have an operator warmup first. Do not
infer production throughput from these short exploratory cases.
Results, memory model and conclusions: FP4_ROUNDOFF_RESULTS.txt (generated after
runs). Raw per-iteration/per-stage records: benchmark-results/fp4-roundoff/.
